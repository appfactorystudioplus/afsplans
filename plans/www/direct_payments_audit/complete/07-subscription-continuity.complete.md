# 07 — Subscription Continuity (complete — server, scheduler and admin; every provider call and the hosted page unverified against a real provider)

## Status

**Complete 2026-10-17** for everything this step puts on the server and in the admin. Nothing is committed. What is **not** proven, listed first:

1. **No provider sandbox was touched.** Every provider call (create a leg with a deferred start, read it back, end a leg at its period end, take that back, cancel, set up a card) goes through the Http client against a fake that records the calls; the order of calls is the evidence. The wire formats follow each provider's documentation. The † items of step 05 (Square plan shape and start date, PayPal inline overrides and Vault) are inherited; new here and equally unverified: **Square's subscription read, cancel and "remove the scheduled cancel"**, and **PayPal's subscription read, cancel and approval status values**.
2. **The hosted renewal page was never opened in a browser.** `resources/views/dp/renew.blade.php` mounts Stripe's payment element, Square's card, or hands over to PayPal; it is plain script, not type-checked, not run.
3. **The four e-mails are sent through a new `DefaultEmailSender` contract whose production implementation (`MjmlDefaultEmailSender`) has never sent anything**: it needs the MJML transform script and the e-mail provider of a real server. The tests substitute a recorder. It builds on the default MJML files that step 15 put in `resources/js/portal/admin/afsemail/mjml-templates/mjml/defaults/dp/`; step 15's own wiring (the "always" flag, the other ten emails, unset brand values — finding E5) is not built, and `MailHandlerSender`'s variable-shape problem (E2) is sidestepped, not fixed.
4. **Pre-filling the page** (name, e-mail, postal code) and **Apple Pay / Google Pay first on Square** (§7.4's "making re-entry easy") are not done. Stripe's payment element shows wallets by itself; Square shows the card form only; PayPal is its own one-tap path.
5. **Discounts that end after N cycles** are not carried: a contract records the canonical discount it has (`discount_reference_num`), the migration carries a lasting discount to all three providers, and `discount_cycles_remaining` exists but nothing fills it. (Step 05 already makes the swap refuse a move that would lose such a discount.)

Verification: on MariaDB 11.4.5, the seven-folder suite — see "Verification" at the end. `tsc --noEmit` clean for `w/www` and the SDK project. MariaDB is stopped.

## Deviations and residue

- **Plan changes are refused only while a migration is *moving*** (the new leg exists), not while a subscriber is merely being asked: otherwise a subscriber who ignores the e-mail (no closing date) could never change plan. A plan change in that window changes the price the migration will create the leg at, because the contract's own price is read when the leg is created.
- **A withdrawn migration cannot be taken back when the old leg is PayPal** (a cancelled PayPal subscription cannot be reactivated): the operator is told and the new leg takes over.
- **`status` vs `contract_status`.** §7.5 says the contract stays `active` throughout, §7.8 says `migrating`; both are delivered: `status` is the provider-normalised word older pages read, `contract_status` is ours and says `migrating`.
- **The scheduler's pass is every 15 minutes** (the plan's cadence), so a hand-over missed by webhooks is found within about 15 minutes after the boundary, and reminders are never earlier than their window by more than that.
- The processor-change **emails are not switchable off** (nothing offers it), as OQ14 requires; there is no per-tenant template customisation yet (step 15).
- Old rows: no back-fill (development). A contract created before this step gets its first leg the first time its provider row changes or a migration is planned.
