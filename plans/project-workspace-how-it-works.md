# Project Workspace — a guide for members

*Managing freelance work you've ordered through the Marketplace.*

---

## What this is

When you hire someone through the Marketplace, you get an order page: what you asked for, what
they've delivered, and a message thread. For a logo or a quick fix, that's all you need.

For bigger work — a few thousand pounds, several months, maybe more than one freelancer — you start
needing different things. A schedule. A view of what's late. A running total of what you've spent
against what you agreed. Some way to see whether being 60% through the budget means you're 60%
through the work.

**Project Workspace** turns a Marketplace order into a real project inside Coordinator, your project
management app. The order keeps working exactly as it does now. On top of it, you get a schedule, a
budget, a team view, and a cost dashboard that updates itself as the work happens.

You don't have to use it. Small orders are usually fine without it.

---

## When it's worth using

Good candidates:

- The work runs longer than a few weeks
- There are several milestones or deliverables
- You've hired more than one freelancer for the same piece of work
- You need to report the cost to someone else — a client, a finance team, a co-founder
- You want a record of the project after the order closes

Probably not worth it:

- One deliverable, one payment, done in a week
- You're already tracking it somewhere else and don't want a second place to look

---

## Before you start

Two things need to be true:

1. **Your plan includes it.** Project Workspace is part of AFS Connect. If your plan doesn't include
   it, you'll see a note on the order page telling you which plan does.
2. **AFS Connect is switched on.** In your Members dashboard, under Settings → AFS Connect, make
   sure the feature is on and **Marketplace Orders** is enabled in the source list.

If either is missing, the "Manage as a project" panel on your order page will tell you which one and
link you to the right place.

## Setting it up

### Step 1 — Open the order

Go to your Marketplace portal, open **My Services** (or **Contracts** for hourly work), and click
the order you want to manage.

### Step 2 — Find "Manage as a project"

It's a panel on the order page. It appears once work has actually started — if the freelancer is
still waiting on your requirements, fill those in first. There's nothing to schedule yet.

### Step 3 — Click create

One button: **"Manage as a project."** Click it and you get a full workspace — a new project with
a schedule, your freelancers, and a budget, all filled in from the order. You'll get a link straight
into it. Setting it up takes a second or two.

*(Adding the order into a project you already have, or tracking the numbers with no schedule at
all, are both things Coordinator can technically do — the button just doesn't offer the choice yet.
If you need one of those specifically, get in touch rather than assuming it's there.)*

---

## What gets created

Coordinator builds:

**A project**, named after what you ordered.

**A task for every milestone**, with the dates from your order and the milestone amount attached.
Milestones show as diamonds on the schedule, the way milestones normally do.

**A task for every thing *you* owe them.** This is the one people don't expect and end up finding
most useful. If the order says you need to supply brand guidelines, or approve a draft, or send
reference material — that becomes a task with a due date, assigned to you, sitting on the same
schedule as everything else. Most late projects are late partly because of something on this list.

**Your freelancers, as people on the project.** They show up as team members you can assign work to,
with their rate attached if it's an hourly contract. They can't log in to your Coordinator — these
are just records so the schedule and budget work properly.

**A budget**, with the agreed amount as the plan and a cost line per milestone.

**A board** with the usual columns, plus two extra ones that match how freelance work actually goes:

- **Awaiting you** — things blocked on your input
- **In review** — delivered, but you haven't approved and paid for it yet

**If it's an hourly contract, this looks a little different.** There's no fixed total and no set
of milestones to turn into tasks — hourly work isn't scoped that way. You still get the project,
the freelancer as a team member with their rate attached, and a budget that fills in as timesheets
are approved, but there's no milestone task list and nothing shows on the board. The schedule and
cost numbers still work — see [The screens](#the-screens) below for what you get instead.

## Keeping it up to date

**You don't.** That's the point.

When the freelancer delivers something, the task moves to "In review". When you approve a milestone,
the task closes and the cost dashboard updates. When you ask for a revision, the task reopens.
Approved hours on an hourly contract appear in the totals. Refunds show up as refunds.

You can change anything about the plan side — dates, who's assigned, priorities, descriptions, your
own extra tasks, notes, tags. That's yours, and nothing overwrites it.

Three things stay under the order's control and get put back if you change them: a milestone task's
**title**, its **status**, and its **percentage complete**. Those aren't opinions, they're facts
about the order, and the order is where they're decided.

Which brings us to the one thing that surprises people:

> **Dragging a milestone card to "Done" doesn't approve it.** Done means paid, and paying is a
> decision with money attached — so it happens deliberately, not by dragging. If you try, you'll get
> a prompt asking whether you'd like to approve it properly, with the amount shown. Say yes and it
> goes through.

For an hourly contract, the equivalent moment is approving a submitted timesheet. That happens on
the freelancer's contract page in the Marketplace, not in Coordinator — Coordinator shows you the
hours and the cost once they're approved, but the approve button itself lives with the order, the
same place the money moves from.

## The screens

### Overview

The landing page. At the top: what you agreed, what you've paid, what's left, and who's on it.

Below that, **the next five things** — a single list combining upcoming milestone deadlines,
anything overdue that's waiting on you, and deliveries sitting unreviewed. Sorted by how urgent it
is, because the real question most mornings is just "what needs me today".

### Schedule

Your normal Gantt chart, filled in from the order. Milestones as diamonds, your own to-dos in their
own colour, arrows where one thing can't start until another finishes.

Underneath the timeline there's a money row showing when payments went out. It's a small thing that
tells you something useful at a glance: if you paid for milestone 2 three weeks before milestone 2
was due to finish, you can see that, rather than having to work it out.

### Cost

Three ways of looking at the same money.

**The waterfall** — agreed → held in escrow → paid out → still to come. One bar, four segments.

**The burn-up** — three lines over time: what you planned to have spent by now, what you've actually
spent, and what's actually been delivered. Where those lines separate is the story. There's also a
forecast line projecting where you'll land if things continue as they are — if it crosses your
budget before your end date, you can see the date it happens.

**The milestone table** — every milestone with its amount, status, estimated versus actual hours,
what it worked out to per hour, and how many revisions it took. Sortable. This is the one people
export and send to their finance team. *(For an hourly contract there's no milestone table to
show — there are no milestones — but the hours and effective-rate figures above it still fill in
from approved timesheets.)*

### People

Who's on the project: name, role, their rate if it's an hourly contract, and whether they're still
active. Today that's what this tab shows — per-participant figures like hours against their cap,
what you've paid them, how fast they turn work around, or how often work comes back for revision
aren't built yet, and the tab says so rather than leaving you to guess. A cross-project capacity
view — seeing that the same freelancer is booked full-time across three of your engagements at
once — is planned for the same reason, but also isn't there yet.

### Risk

A short list of things worth a look, each with what triggered it:

- **Milestone at risk** — due within five days, less than halfway done
- **Waiting on you** — something you owe them is overdue
- **Gone quiet** — no delivery, milestone activity or approved timesheet for a week (this one
  doesn't yet see messages — a freelancer who's actively messaging but hasn't delivered or
  logged anything could still trip it)
- **Spending ahead of progress** — you've paid out noticeably more than has been delivered
- **Revisions piling up** — three or more on one milestone, or revisions eating a big share of what
  you've paid
- **Hours cap** — an hourly freelancer's approved hours for the current week have already reached
  80% of their weekly limit. This only counts hours once you've approved the timesheet, so it's a
  same-week warning rather than a live one — a very full week can go past the cap before its
  timesheet is even submitted, let alone approved
- **Not syncing** — the Marketplace and Coordinator have lost touch (rare; usually clears itself)

"Waiting on you" appearing in a list you opened to check on your freelancer is intentional.

### Order actions

Right now, **approving a milestone** is the one order action you can do from inside Coordinator —
click Approve on a milestone task and it says exactly what it'll do before you confirm:

> *Approve M1 — releases £6,000.00 to Ana P. This cannot be undone.*

The Marketplace is still where it actually happens; Coordinator just gives you the button.

Accepting a delivery, requesting a revision, approving a timesheet, and sending a message all still
happen on the order page in the Marketplace, not in Coordinator — the schedule and cost dashboard
reflect them once they happen, but the actions themselves aren't in Coordinator yet.

## What the numbers mean

Most of the dashboard is self-explanatory. These four aren't, and they're the useful ones.

**Cost efficiency.** Compares what's been delivered against what you've paid. Above 1.0 means you're
getting more than you've paid for so far — usually because you're paying on milestones and work is
running ahead. Below 1.0 means you've paid ahead of delivery. Neither is automatically bad; a
sustained slide downward is worth asking about.

**Schedule efficiency.** Compares what's been delivered against what the plan said would be done by
now. Below 1.0 means behind schedule.

**Forecast final cost.** Takes your agreed budget and adjusts it by how efficiently things have gone
so far. If it's above your budget, that's the projected overrun. It's a projection, not a promise —
early in a project it swings around a lot. It settles down and becomes worth trusting somewhere
around a third of the way in.

**Effective hourly rate.** What each hour of delivered work actually cost you. On fixed-price
milestones you never see an hourly rate — this reconstructs one. It's how you tell a £6,000
milestone that took twenty hours from a £6,000 milestone that took two hundred. Over a few projects,
it's the single most useful number for deciding who to hire again.

There's also **revision cost** — how much of what you've paid went into milestones that needed doing
more than once. It's usually a fair measure of how clear the original brief was, and people who
watch it tend to write better briefs.

## Working with your freelancers

By default, freelancers can see the schedule for **their own tasks** — dates, what depends on what,
status and progress, and what's coming. They see it on their own order page in the Marketplace;
they don't get access to your Coordinator. It's read-only on their side — updating progress or
status still happens the way it always has, through delivering work and submitting timesheets on
the order itself, not by editing anything on this schedule view.

They **cannot** see your budget, what you're paying anyone else, other freelancers' work, or any of
your own internal tasks. Even with sharing on.

You can turn sharing off per project, but it's on by default for a reason: a freelancer who can't
see the plan can't follow it.

## Common questions

**Does this cost extra?**
It's part of AFS Connect. Depending on your plan there may be a limit on how many active workspaces
you can have at once — you'll see it if you hit it.

**What if I stop using it?**
Nothing breaks. Switch AFS Connect off and the project stays exactly where it is — it's your data,
in your workspace. It just stops updating itself from the order.

**What happens when the order finishes?**
The project stays. It stops syncing, but the schedule, the costs, the deliverables and the whole
history remain in your workspace. That's often the main reason to have set it up.

**Can I set one up after the order is already done?**
Yes. Plenty of people do — you get the record without having had to keep it during the project.

**What if I cancel or get a refund?**
The project is archived, never deleted. A cancelled project is still a thing that happened and still
cost you time, and the cost view shows the refund rather than pretending it didn't.

**Can I put several orders into one project?**
Not yet from the order page itself — right now, "Manage as a project" always creates its own new
workspace. Combining several orders — say, three freelancers on one build — into a single project
is planned but isn't available from the button today. Get in touch if this is what you need.

**What if I delete the project?**
It stays deleted. It won't quietly reappear the next time the order updates. If you change your
mind, the order page will offer to create a fresh one.

**A freelancer left partway through. What happens to their work?**
Their tasks stay, their hours stay, and the record of what you paid them stays. They stop showing up
as someone you can assign new work to. Nothing is erased — that history is part of what the project
cost.

**Can freelancers see each other?**
No.

## If something looks wrong

**The workspace panel isn't on my order page.**
Usually one of three things: work hasn't started yet (fill in your requirements first), AFS Connect
is off, or your plan doesn't include it. The panel says which.

**The numbers look out of date.**
Check the Risk list for "Not syncing". The Marketplace and Coordinator reconcile themselves once a
day, so a brief gap usually fixes itself. If it's still there tomorrow, get in touch.

**A task I closed reopened by itself.**
That'll be a milestone. Milestone status follows the order, and closed means paid — see
[Keeping it up to date](#keeping-it-up-to-date). Approve it from the order actions panel and it'll
close properly.

**I can't approve a milestone from Coordinator.**
The Marketplace has rules about what can happen when — a milestone can't be approved before the
freelancer submits it, for instance. Coordinator will show you the Marketplace's own explanation of
what's blocking it.

**My budget totals don't match what I paid.**
The cost dashboard includes everything you've attached to the project, not just freelance spend —
software, internal time, expenses. The milestone table shows freelance spend on its own if that's
what you're after.

## In short

Order the work in the Marketplace. Click one button. Get a schedule, a budget, and a cost dashboard
that keeps itself current — and a record that's still yours after the order closes.
